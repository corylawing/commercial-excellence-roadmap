When a lead is released to Salesforce

A new prospect lives in HubSpot and is handed to Sales only when the scoring model releases it.

Nurture

070100160

Total score = Fit (up to 60) + Engagement (up to 100, last 90 days), so 160 at most.

  • Nurture0–69No release. Marketing keeps nurturing.Lives in HubSpot. In Salesforce only as an unreleased lead, if they join a campaign
  • Warm70–99Marketing may release, with one tick.HubSpot, then Salesforce once released
  • Hot100+Released automatically, within 15 minutes.Salesforce, as MQL
  1. The score is split. Salesforce scores fit, events and requests; HubSpot scores email clicks, visits and downloads and sends one HubSpot Digital Score. Salesforce adds them into the total. Why: each system scores what it can see, and HubSpot cannot read campaign member status. HubSpot may take over the whole score later.
  2. Salesforce shows three fields: Fit Score, Engagement Score and Total Score. Sales sees all three, as the model asks.
  3. Release happens in Salesforce. Every HubSpot person arrives as an unreleased lead (no alert, no rep queue). Hot releases automatically; Warm when Marketing ticks Release to Sales in HubSpot. HubSpot sees the score and the release status.
  4. A released lead arrives as MQL. Within one business day Sales accepts it as SQL or returns it with a required reason. The queue will sit in Noodle's Today.
  5. A returned or Closed Lost lead keeps its fit points, loses its engagement points and must qualify again.

The tracking code goes on the website in week 1 because the engagement score counts the last 90 days. The sooner it collects, the sooner a score means something.

Campaigns

Salesforce stays the one place campaigns and their members live, because Sales has no HubSpot access. HubSpot writes its registrations into them and reads attendance back.

WhatWhere it is madeHow the other side gets it
The campaignSalesforce for events, webinars and Sales campaigns; HubSpot for email, digital and paidA small job makes its twin in the other system
A registration or response HubSpot capturesHubSpotA HubSpot rule adds the person to the Salesforce campaign with the right status
Webinar attendanceZoom writes it to Salesforce, as todayFields on the person carry it to HubSpot
In-person attendance, uploaded lists, members reps addSalesforce, as todayFields on the person carry it to HubSpot
  1. Two doors, one per kind of campaign. Events, webinars and Sales campaigns are created in Salesforce, as today: they need attendance, the partner and the parent event, which only Salesforce holds. Email, digital and paid campaigns are created in HubSpot, from the forms phase. Why one door per kind: the same campaign created in both tools is a duplicate nobody can untangle.
  2. Each member has one writer. HubSpot writes what it captures. Zoom, the Attendance button and reps keep writing what they write today. Why: two tools setting the same member's status is where campaign counts drift.
  3. A member must exist in Salesforce. So a campaign response creates the person there as an unreleased lead: no alert, no rep's queue, not MQL. Release still follows the score.
  4. HubSpot writes only the campaign's own statuses. Salesforce silently ignores a status the campaign lacks and leaves the member on Invited. That already happens with webinar sign-ups.
  5. Email sends and opens stop becoming members (recommended). They are 83% of new members today, most of them written by Pardot. Email results stay in HubSpot; Salesforce campaigns keep the responses Sales acts on.

Whichever door is used, a small job makes the twin in the other system and ties the two together. The job is ours to build, in the forms phase: HubSpot's connector only uses Salesforce campaigns that already exist. Until then every campaign is created in Salesforce, which Pardot needs anyway.

The round trip is proven on one test campaign in the pilot (step 15). It is then switched on campaign by campaign, as each one's emails and forms move to HubSpot. Until then Pardot keeps writing members as today.

Not confirmed in HubSpot's pages: that its rule can choose the member status. A HubSpot form on its own always writes "Responded". Step 15 settles it; the fallback is a small Salesforce rule that sets the status.

How we stay out of each other's way

  1. One owner per system. Daria owns Salesforce, Sergio owns HubSpot, and Cory owns what crosses between them: the connector's settings, the field map and the data moves. Anyone building in another owner's system tells them first. Why: neither system merges two people's changes; the last save wins silently.
  2. Start only when you are Ready. Begin a step only once the steps it waits for are ticked here. Tick yours when it is done and checked, not when Claude says it is. Cory's lead session reads both systems before ticking a gate. Why: everyone can see who is free to start, and a Claude session reports success more easily than it proves it.
  3. One field map, one editor. It lists every synced field, its direction and which system wins. Cory's lead session edits it; Daria and Sergio ask for changes. Why: the sync is only as good as this table, and three editors make three versions.
  4. Separate folders, separate keys. The project gets its own shared folder, one sub-folder per lane, and each lane its own HubSpot key. Why: the Salesforce code folder has no shared copy and other sessions already work in it; one key per lane means every change traces to one lane and can be switched off alone.
  5. Pilot before volume. Every setting and load runs on the pilot first. Why: a wrong mapping on 50 people is a fix; on 83,000 it is a clean-up project.

Why we build it this way

  1. HubSpot's own connector, not a sync we write. HubSpot maintains it, and it already handles retries, limits and matching people by email. A hand-built sync is the part that takes months.
  2. Salesforce wins on every shared field. Sync problems come from two systems editing the same field. So each field has one owner, and HubSpot writes only into its own Salesforce fields: its digital score, the Release to Sales tick, the request date, last marketing touch and bounced.
  3. HubSpot connects as Cory's Salesforce user. Cory's decision. His login sees every record, so the pilot is held by HubSpot's sync list and test practices are kept out by their flag. Only one rule skips his changes (the doctor name check); lead routing runs.
  4. HubSpot email events never become interactions in Salesforce. That setting writes one entry per open and per click, with no de-duplication. Pardot did the same, and we now delete 2.4 million of those entries. They would also inflate Engagement and Overall activity.
  5. While both tools run, unsubscribes travel through Salesforce. Pardot and HubSpot do not talk to each other. Both read and write the same opt-out box in Salesforce, so an unsubscribe in one stops email from the other.

Loaded events show on each person's timeline and can trigger automation. They will not appear in HubSpot's own email reports, which count only emails HubSpot sent.

Not confirmed: whether loaded Pardot events can count in HubSpot's score. If they cannot, click and visit points build up from week 1 only.
